Findings

The critique is right about the mechanism and wrong about the absolutes. The system it describes is a language model inside a loop of ordinary code; the model does real work inside that loop, which the critique denies; and the label is loose in ways that analysts have documented. The evidence read does not show that the economics are anything worse than hype.

11.1 Claims the evidence supports

A model predicts one token at a time and cannot act on the world by itself; vendors document exactly this [S13] [S14]. Its explanations of its own steps can be false [S1], its accuracy can drop sharply under small perturbations [S5], it generalises worse than people by the account of a scaling pioneer [S52], and its reliability at a high success rate is far below its reliability at a low one [S25]. The executor half of an agent is decades-old engineering. Marketing has inflated the word “agent”, and an analyst firm has a name for it [S37]. A study widely read as showing that most enterprise pilots fail exists, with limits [S35].

11.2 Claims the evidence does not support

“No AI involved” fails on the model itself. “No reasoning” and “no maths” are contradicted by causal evidence of multi-step computation inside a model [S1] [S2] and by gains on checkable tasks [S6]. The restaurant demo was a vision-based browser agent and not an API script [S56]. The claim that every head researcher quit over a dead end fails against the record [S53] [S55] [S54]. Private capital has not run out [S46] [S49]. The securities-law claim has no support in any legal record found. Proponents have their own gaps: the benchmarks that headline agent progress have been retired or questioned [S21], productivity evidence is unsettled [S27], and several of the sources used here are the vendors’ own (section 1).

11.4 Recommendations and open questions

Use the model where input is untidy and the answer can be checked; keep it away from anything that moves equipment; measure success at the run length you need; and keep the exit cost low while the evidence settles. Open questions remain: the true size of the productivity effect, how well verification holds up when the checker shares the generator’s blind spots, and what the 2026 benchmark scores really are. A later edition will revisit them.
