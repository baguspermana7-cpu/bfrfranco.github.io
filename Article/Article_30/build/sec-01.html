The 14 claims tested

The case against “agentic AI” usually runs like this: a language model only guesses the next token, an agent is ordinary code around it, and the label exists to sell a story to investors. Each part of that argument is a claim that can be checked. This article states fourteen of them, C1 to C14, and grades each against primary sources: documentation, research and benchmarks for the mechanism; filings and earnings for the money; definitions for the names. No claim is accepted or rejected on who makes it.

Each kind needs different evidence. Mechanism claims (C1–C7, C10, C11) are settled by documentation, interpretability research and benchmarks, in sections 2 to 6. Money claims (C8, C9, C12, C13) are settled by filings and earnings, in section 7. Naming claims (C6, C7, C14) are a matter of definition, not measurement, in section 8. Sections 9 and 10 turn the findings into design rules and a checklist for a data-center team. Article 29 explains the model itself, including sampling and post-training.

Terms follow common usage: an agent is a model running in a loop with tools; an agentic system includes the code around it.
