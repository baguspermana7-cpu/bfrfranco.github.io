Follow the money: the “IPO story” claim

The video makes an economic argument: AI companies have exhausted private money, need to list on public markets, and need a story to sell, and the story is agents (E12). It adds that the researchers have left because language models are a dead end (E9) and that the field has spent “$3 trillion” (E8). These are claims about money and people, and they can be checked against filings, earnings releases and press reports. The findings are mixed. The spending is very large and partly debt-funded, the IPO motive is real, but the claim that private money has run out is contradicted by the largest private rounds on record, and the researcher claim fails as stated.

7.3 The “wall”

Sutskever said at NeurIPS 2024: “Pre-training as we know it will unquestionably end… We’ve achieved peak data and there’ll be no more… There’s only one internet.” He predicted the next models would “be agentic in a real ways” [S53]. In November 2025 he described the field as “moving from the age of scaling to the age of research” [S54]. Projections put exhaustion of the stock of public human text between 2026 and 2032, while training compute grows about 5× a year (Article 29, section 9) [S62].

Against a hard wall: METR horizons doubled roughly every four months [S26] [S27], and revenue run-rates grew several-fold in a year [S46]. The two views reconcile as follows. Pre-training on human text is meeting limits, and the gains moved to reinforcement learning, test-time compute and tool use. That is why the industry talks about agents. Part of the video’s story has a technical counterpart: agents are a new scaling axis and not only a narrative.

7.4 Who left, and why

  • Yann LeCun left Meta (reported November 2025) to found a world-model start-up. He “has previously called LLMs a ‘dead end’” and said “We’re never going to get to human-level A.I. by just training on text” [S55]. This supports E9 for Meta.
  • Ilya Sutskever left OpenAI in 2024. He argues that generalisation, not tooling, is the open problem [S53] [S54]. That departure is outside “the last year”.
  • John Jumper, a 2024 Nobel laureate, left Google DeepMind for Anthropic (reported 2026-06-19) [S57]: a move between labs.
  • Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI’s chief scientist, co-authored a June 2026 OpenAI plan with Sam Altman. I saw this only in a page listing (the page itself was not readable), so his current title is reported, unverified; I found no evidence that he left [S63].
  • Safety departures, September 2026. Joe Benton, who led a safety team at Anthropic, Josh Engels of Google and Jacob Coxon of Anthropic left, citing fears that progress could become “uncontrollable”; “There are no adults in the room” [S56]. Their stated reason points the opposite way to the video: too much capability too fast, and no mention of a dead end.

E9, “all of them quit because LLMs are a dead end”, is unsupported as stated. One prominent case fits, several do not.

7.5 Enterprise reality

MIT’s NANDA initiative reported that “despite $30–40 billion in enterprise investment into GenAI… 95% of organizations are getting zero return”. For task-specific tools, “sixty percent… evaluated… only 20 percent reached pilot stage and just 5 percent reached production”, with the root cause given as “the learning gap, tools that don’t learn, integrate poorly, or match workflows”. The method was 300+ public initiatives, 52 interviews and 153 survey responses, January to June 2025, with success defined as “marked and sustained productivity and/or P&L impact” [S37]. Press coverage turned it into “95% of pilots failing” [S38]. Reading the report itself, the limits are visible: small samples recruited at conferences, a strict P&L definition of “return”, a 95% that applies to integrated task-specific pilots and not all use, and authors whose own project promotes an agentic architecture, so the report argues for agents that learn and is not an anti-agent finding.

Gartner stated on 2025-06-25 that “over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027”. I confirmed the headline only; the release page was not readable, so its reasons, its estimate of how many vendors are genuine, and its forecasts are unverified [S39]. Gartner also popularised the term agent washing for relabelling assistants, RPA and chatbots as agents [S39].

Weighing the economics

Bull evidence: revenue growth [S46] [S47], the capability trend [S26] [S27], harness-driven gains [S25]. Bear evidence: pilot failure rates [S37], cancellation forecasts [S39], debt-funded capex [S40] [S44], benchmark contamination [S23], unstable productivity evidence [S28] [S29], valuation critiques [S50]. For an operations buyer: marketing inflation is real, so size every deployment on your own measured success rate, and keep the exit cost low.
