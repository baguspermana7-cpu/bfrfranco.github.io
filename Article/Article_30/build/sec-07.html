Investment, revenue and the IPO claim

The video makes an economic argument: AI companies have exhausted private money, need to list on public markets, and need a story to sell, and the story is agents (E12). It adds that the researchers have left because language models are a dead end (E9) and that the field has spent “$3 trillion” (E8). Checked against filings, earnings releases and press reports: spending is very large and partly debt-funded; the IPO motive is real; “private money has run out” is contradicted by the largest private rounds on record; the researcher claim fails as stated.

7.3 Is scaling hitting a wall?

Sutskever said at NeurIPS 2024: “Pre-training as we know it will unquestionably end… We’ve achieved peak data and there’ll be no more… There’s only one internet.” He predicted the next models would “be agentic in a real ways” [S53]. In November 2025 he described the field as “moving from the age of scaling to the age of research” [S54]. Projections put exhaustion of the stock of public human text between 2026 and 2032, while training compute grows about 5× a year (Article 29, section 9) [S62].

Against a hard wall: METR horizons doubled roughly every four months [S26] [S27], and revenue run-rates grew several-fold in a year [S46]. Both hold: pre-training on human text is meeting limits, and gains moved to reinforcement learning, test-time compute and tool use. That is why the industry talks about agents: they are a new scaling axis, not only a narrative.

7.5 Enterprise adoption: MIT NANDA and Gartner

MIT’s NANDA initiative reported that “despite $30–40 billion in enterprise investment into GenAI… 95% of organizations are getting zero return”. For task-specific tools, “sixty percent… evaluated… only 20 percent reached pilot stage and just 5 percent reached production”, with the root cause given as “the learning gap, tools that don’t learn, integrate poorly, or match workflows”. The method was 300+ public initiatives, 52 interviews and 153 survey responses, January to June 2025, with success defined as “marked and sustained productivity and/or P&L impact” [S37]. Press coverage turned it into “95% of pilots failing” [S38]. Limits visible in the report itself: small samples recruited at conferences, a strict P&L definition of “return”, a 95% that applies to integrated task-specific pilots and not all use, and authors whose own project promotes an agentic architecture, so the report argues for agents that learn and is not an anti-agent finding.

Gartner, 2025-06-25: “over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027”. Headline confirmed only; the release page was not readable, so its reasons, its estimate of how many vendors are genuine, and its forecasts are unverified [S39]. Gartner also popularised the term agent washing for relabelling assistants, RPA and chatbots as agents [S39].

Bull evidence: revenue growth [S46] [S47], the capability trend [S26] [S27], harness-driven gains [S25].
