The name: is it “AI”, is it an “agent”?

Whether “agentic AI” is an honest name depends on which definition of the two words you use, and the published definitions disagree about where the line lies. The dispute is about vocabulary, and no measurement can settle it. What can be said is which definition each party is using, and how regulators treat the question. They tend to regulate the system and its risks, not the label on the box.

8.2 The proposed rename

The video proposes “generative computing” or “automated computing” and asks to “get AI out of the name” (E14) [S1]. There are three points to weigh.

  • The video’s own description concedes that “the language model is AI” [S1]. So the disagreement narrows to the word on the whole system, not on the model.
  • By the textbook definition the whole system is an agent [S21], and by Anthropic’s definition it is a workflow or an agent depending on who picks the next step [S14]. “No AI in agentic AI” is therefore a definitional claim and not a factual one.
  • “Generative computing” names the drafter, not the loop. A more exact engineering label for the architecture the video describes is LLM-orchestrated automation: the model is the planner or router, and code is the executor. That label is this article’s suggestion, not an industry term.

The marketing critique inside the rename has substance. Gartner’s “agent washing” [S39] and the high pilot failure rates [S37] show that the label is applied loosely. A reader should ask any vendor which of the six levels in section 4 the product is, and which decisions the model makes. The owner’s counter-claim C6, that the video’s verdict reduces a system to its mechanism, is supported with a caveat: describing a mechanism is not the same as deceiving anyone, though the label inflation is documented.

8.3 A note on the CISA page

When I read the CISA page for the OT guidance on 2026-10-16, it displayed the title as “Super Intelligence in Operational Technology” with “SI” throughout, while the URL slug and the PDF say “Artificial Intelligence” [S33]. This article cites the PDF title: Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology. I report the discrepancy and draw no conclusion from it.

Procurement question

Do not argue about the noun. Ask what the system can read, what it can write, who approves a write, and what happens when the model is wrong. Those four answers describe the risk identically under any name.
