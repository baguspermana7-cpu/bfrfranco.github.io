Are “AI” and “agent” accurate names?

Whether “agentic AI” is an accurate name depends on the definition used, and published definitions draw the line in different places. No measurement settles a vocabulary question. Below: the definition each source uses, and how regulators treat it (they regulate the system and its risks, not the label).

8.2 The video’s proposed rename

The video proposes “generative computing” or “automated computing” and asks to “get AI out of the name” (E14) [S1].

  • The video’s own description concedes that “the language model is AI” [S1]. The disagreement is about the word for the whole system, not the model.
  • By the textbook definition the whole system is an agent [S21], and by Anthropic’s definition it is a workflow or an agent depending on who picks the next step [S14]. “No AI in agentic AI” is a definitional claim, not a factual one.
  • “Generative computing” names the drafter, not the loop. A more exact engineering label for the architecture the video describes is LLM-orchestrated automation: the model is the planner or router, and code is the executor. (This article’s suggestion, not an industry term.)

The marketing critique has substance. Gartner’s “agent washing” [S39] and the high pilot failure rates [S37] show that the label is applied loosely. Ask any vendor which of the six levels in section 4 the product is, and which decisions the model makes. The author’s counter-claim C6, that the video’s verdict reduces a system to its mechanism, is supported with a caveat: describing a mechanism is not the same as deceiving anyone, though the label inflation is documented.

8.3 Title discrepancy on the CISA page

On 2026-10-16 the CISA page for the OT guidance displayed the title as “Super Intelligence in Operational Technology” with “SI” throughout, while the URL slug and the PDF say “Artificial Intelligence” [S33]. This article cites the PDF title: Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology. The discrepancy is reported without conclusion.

Procurement question

Ask four questions: what can the system read, what can it write, who approves a write, what happens when the model is wrong. The answers describe the risk under any name.
