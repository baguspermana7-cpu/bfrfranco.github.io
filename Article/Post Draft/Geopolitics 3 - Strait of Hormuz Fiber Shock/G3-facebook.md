New on ResistanceZero: Strait of Hormuz Fiber Shock: Engineering Impact Analysis.

What happens to a region's internet if several submarine cables in the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman are damaged at once?

Our Geopolitics 3 article is an engineering-first scenario analysis built from public sources. It assigns blame to no one. Submarine cables carry more than 99% of intercontinental data, and 20.7 million barrels of oil a day moved through Hormuz in 2024.

The finding: the likely failure is regional degradation, not an instant blackout. Traffic reroutes quickly, but longer detours add a practical 15-40 ms of latency and the surviving routes lose spare capacity. Repair is slow, with a recent global average of about 1.5 months before work can even start.

The article compares how different Gulf states absorb the shock and what real resilience looks like, including physical route separation. A scenario calculator is included.
