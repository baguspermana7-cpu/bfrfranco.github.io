# X / Twitter — Geopolitics 3: Strait of Hormuz Fiber Shock (thread)

1/ What happens to the network if several submarine cables in the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman fail at once? An engineering-first, scenario-based thread. It attributes blame to no one. 🧵

2/ Why this corridor: 20.7 million barrels a day moved through Hormuz in 2024 (US EIA), so an incident here is read as an energy event too. Submarine cables carry more than 99% of intercontinental data.

3/ Logical route counts can mislead. TeleGeography notes territorial-routing constraints push many paths into Omani waters, so systems that look diverse share a failure domain, like a single-line diagram hiding one.

4/ What breaks first is not a blackout. Carriers reroute and hyperscalers rebalance quickly. Then surviving routes carry traffic they were not designed to carry continuously, and it becomes a margin problem: spare capacity, then clean latency, then stable application behavior.

5/ The detour costs a practical 15-40 ms of RTT when traffic moves onto longer westbound or terrestrial paths. That is enough to hurt replication and cross-border applications before any outage appears.

6/ Repair is the long pole. The recent global average delay before repair work can even start is about 1.5 months.

7/ GCC states differ. Those with exits outside the Strait or toward the Red Sea absorb shocks better; those deeper inside the Gulf face prolonged congestion. New systems add choices, not fewer chokepoints.

8/ Time horizon decides the story: six hours, six days or six weeks. The decisive variable is how long the corridor runs degraded before repair can begin.
