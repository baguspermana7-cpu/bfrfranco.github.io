The most common reason a data center commissioning program runs late is not test failure. It is defect management: open items that are not tracked rigorously enough to surface as gate blockers until the customer witness date is two weeks away.

The RFS Readiness Workbench was built to make gate-state visible and defect aging explicit from G0 onward.

---

What the workbench covers:

Gate board: G0 through G7 gates across the commissioning sequence, with gate-state readiness indicators and the ability to track open, closed, and deferred items per gate.

Defect aging summary: a running view of defect age by severity and gate, so the team can see at a glance which items are approaching threshold age without a manual tally.

Evidence management: attachment and reference tracking for test evidence, certificates, and third-party reports linked to the gate they satisfy.

Customer witness pack: the compiled evidence set prepared for the customer-witnessed test phase, with status tracking per item.

Forecast summary: RFS date projection based on current defect burn-down rate and gate-state, so the commercial team is working from the same forecast as the commissioning team.

---

The gate-driven model is not just a workflow preference. It is the structure that makes RFS forecast reliability possible: if every defect is tagged to the gate where it was raised and the gate where it must be resolved, the forecast can be derived from data rather than subjective judgment about "how things are going."
