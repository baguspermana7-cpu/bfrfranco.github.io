Title: Why Data Center RFS Dates Slip — And the Gate Structure That Prevents It

---

Data center commissioning programs slip for predictable reasons. The test schedule is usually not the primary one. The primary causes are defect aging — items that were raised at G3 and are still open at G5 — and evidence gaps that surface during the customer witness preparation, after everyone has been told the program is on track.

Both of those failures have the same structural root: defect tracking and evidence management that is disconnected from the gate sequence. When a commissioning team is managing open items in a flat spreadsheet with no gate linkage, the question "what is blocking G6?" requires manual analysis to answer. When the customer asks the same question during a progress meeting, the answer is usually qualified or deferred.

The RFS Readiness Workbench is a gate-driven tracker that makes gate-state and defect aging the primary view, not a derived one.

The gate structure runs G0 through G7 — from design freeze through to energization, functional testing, integrated system tests, customer witness, and final handover. Each gate has a readiness indicator that reflects the open-defect and outstanding-evidence state for items assigned to that gate. The defect aging summary shows how long each open item has been in the system by severity and gate assignment. The evidence management layer links test reports, certificates, and third-party documents to the gate where they are required.

The customer witness pack view assembles the evidence and test record set that needs to be presented at the witnessed phase, with a status indicator per item. This is the view that prevents the last-minute discovery that a tier-qualified test report is still outstanding.

The forecast summary derives an RFS date estimate from the current defect burn-down rate and gate-state. This is not a guarantee — it is a data-derived projection that makes the commercial team's schedule assumptions visible against the engineering reality.

For commissioning managers, the value is in having a single source of truth that links defects, evidence, and gate-state. For project directors and commercial leads, the value is in RFS forecasts that are grounded in gate-state rather than optimism.
