One pattern I have seen repeatedly in data center commissioning programs: the project is reported as "on track" until about three weeks before the customer witness date, when it becomes clear that several defects raised at early gates are still open and a few critical test reports have not been signed off yet.

The problem is almost always structural — defect tracking and evidence management that is disconnected from the gate sequence. When everything is in a flat list, the question "what is blocking G6?" requires manual analysis to answer, and manual analysis usually happens too late.

I built the RFS Readiness Workbench to change that structure. It tracks defects against the gate where they were raised and the gate where they must be resolved. The defect aging summary makes items that have been open too long visible immediately. The customer witness pack view shows exactly which evidence is still outstanding before the witness date arrives as a surprise.

It also generates an RFS forecast from the current defect burn-down and gate-state rather than from subjective judgment about progress.

The tool is at resistancezero.com/rfs-readiness-workbench.html.
