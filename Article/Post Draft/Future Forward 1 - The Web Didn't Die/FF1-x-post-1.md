# X / Twitter — Future Forward 1: The Web Didn't Die — It Split (thread)

1/ The web did not die. It split. Domains, traffic and browsers are all still growing, while AI answers take the click. A thread on who wins the next interface shift, with the numbers. 🧵

2/ The web is not shrinking. Verisign: 386.9 million domain registrations at the end of 2025. Cloudflare Radar: internet traffic grew 19% in 2025. WordPress still powers 42.4% of all websites (W3Techs, March 2026).

3/ Search is not dead either. Google held 90.89% of worldwide search share in December 2025 (Statcounter), and Chrome holds roughly 71.25% of browsers. What changed is how much of the need is met before any site earns the click.

4/ AI Overviews passed 1 billion monthly users in 2024 and reached 1.5 billion across 200 countries by Google I/O 2025. Search volume can rise while publisher traffic weakens.

5/ Pew Research Center: on pages with an AI summary, users clicked a traditional result link 8% of the time, against 15% without one. Clicks on links inside the summary itself: 1%.

6/ The split: pages that can be summarized, paraphrased or absorbed by a feed or an answer lose. Sites people or machines still need after the answer win: products, workflows, official systems, tools, dashboards, transactions.

7/ Under most pressure: definitions, low-friction explainers, generic affiliate copy, thin SEO pages. The consequences reach media economics first, then B2B, where documentation, specs and procurement need a durable, owned home.

8/ 2026-2028 is about compression: fewer pages get meaningful attention. Long term, 2028-2035 rewards fewer pages, better systems and stronger direct audiences. The test: be the place a user or agent still needs after the answer.
