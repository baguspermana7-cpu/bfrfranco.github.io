## The web did not die. It split.

If the web were truly dying, domains, traffic and browser activity would contract together. They are not. Verisign counted 386.9 million domain registrations at the end of 2025. Cloudflare Radar saw internet traffic grow 19% in 2025. W3Techs shows WordPress powering 42.4% of all websites in March 2026. Statcounter has Google at 90.89% of worldwide search share and Chrome at roughly 71.25% of browsers.

What changed is how much of a user's need is met before any external site earns the click. AI Overviews passed 1 billion monthly users in 2024 and reached 1.5 billion across 200 countries by Google I/O 2025. Pew Research Center found that on pages with an AI summary, users clicked a traditional result link 8% of the time, against 15% for pages without one, and clicked a link inside the summary itself only 1% of the time. Similarweb recorded AI referral traffic of 1.13 billion visits in June 2025, still far smaller than Google's. Search volume can rise while publisher traffic weakens.

The article argues the web is splitting into two classes. The weakest model is the commodity page: definitions, low-friction explainers, generic affiliate copy, thin SEO pages. The strongest sites act like products, systems or communities: tools, dashboards, transactions, official systems and trusted knowledge that users or machines still need after the answer.

The consequences run beyond SEO. Media economics come first, since generic publishing is harder to finance when each click is harder to earn. In B2B and operational contexts, documentation, specifications, procurement and compliance material still need a durable, owned home, even if AI becomes the way people discover it.

The forecast: 2026-2028 is about compression, with fewer pages receiving meaningful attention. 2028-2035 rewards fewer pages, better systems and stronger direct audiences. The article ends with an interactive Website Resilience Calculator for estimating traffic and revenue exposure.
