# LinkedIn — Future Forward 1: The Web Didn't Die — It Split

The web is not dying. It is being repriced, and the numbers say so.

Verisign counted 386.9 million domain registrations at the end of 2025. Cloudflare Radar saw internet traffic grow 19% that year. WordPress still powers 42.4% of all websites (W3Techs, March 2026). Google held 90.89% of worldwide search share in December 2025, and Chrome roughly 71.25% of browsers.

What changed is the path. AI Overviews reached 1.5 billion users across 200 countries by Google I/O 2025. Pew Research Center found that on pages with an AI summary, users clicked a traditional result link 8% of the time against 15% without one, and clicked a link inside the summary only 1% of the time. Search volume can rise while publisher traffic falls.

Future Forward 1 argues the web is splitting into two classes. Commodity pages (definitions, thin explainers, generic affiliate copy) can be absorbed by an answer. Sites that act as products, systems or communities, with tools, transactions and trusted documentation, are still needed after the answer.

It sets out 2026-2028 as a period of compression, then 2028-2035 as one that rewards fewer pages and stronger direct audiences. An interactive Website Resilience Calculator estimates your own traffic and revenue exposure.
