Something that often gets skipped in data center site selection discussions in Indonesia is the actual topology of the grid you are connecting to. Not just the voltage level or the substation name — the full picture of how the transmission network is structured and where the concentration risks are.

The Java-Bali grid carries about 70% of Indonesia's national peak demand on a single synchronous island chain. That has real implications for redundancy design at critical facilities.

I put together a browser-based map and single-line diagram of the entire Jamali transmission system — 744 substations and 488 transmission edges across the 500, 275, 150, and 70 kV backbone. Five provincial sub-pages add a 20 kV overlay showing data-center operators, industrial intakes, and how each major power plant connects to the backbone.

The data is sourced from PLN AR 2024, RUPTL 2025-2034, and OpenStreetMap transmission infrastructure, so it is publicly grounded rather than speculative.

It is at resistancezero.com/pln-java-grid.html if you are working on grid-resilience analysis, facility siting, or just want to understand the system you are depending on.
