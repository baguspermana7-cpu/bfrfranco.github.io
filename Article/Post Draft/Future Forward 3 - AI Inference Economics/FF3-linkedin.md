# LinkedIn — Future Forward 3: AI Inference Economics

Training builds the model once. Inference runs it every time a user sends a prompt, an API call executes or an agent takes an action. FF-3 argues that is where AI economics now live.

By 2026, two-thirds of AI compute is inference. Jensen Huang said at GTC 2025 that 70% of NVIDIA's data center revenue comes from inference-optimized chips. API prices have collapsed: frontier-quality inference fell 92% in 14 months between GPT-4 and GPT-4o, and 99.5% to GPT-4o-mini.

The constraint is energy. SemiAnalysis projects 93.3 GW of inference-specific power demand by 2030. The EIA puts AI data centers at about 4% of US electricity in 2025, up from 2.5% in 2023. Training a frontier model is a 50-100 GWh one-time event; inference for the same model runs every hour of every day.

Economics then filter which products survive: revenue per request must beat cost per request. GitHub Copilot reportedly lost an average of $20 per user per month in 2023 and reached profitability by mid-2025. The article also covers edge versus cloud placement, US export controls aimed at inference chips and the next three years of deployment.
