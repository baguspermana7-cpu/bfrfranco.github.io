Title: ASEAN Data Center 2026 — Six Markets, One Report, No Shortcuts

---

Southeast Asia's data center market does not behave like a single region. Singapore operates under a capacity moratorium with a managed pipeline release and among the strictest PUE and sustainability requirements anywhere in Asia. Indonesia is in the middle of a grid expansion cycle, has no moratorium, and carries an emission factor that makes green claims complicated. Malaysia has become the hyperscaler landing zone of choice since Singapore tightened, with Johor hosting a cluster that would count among the world's largest if concentrated in a single city. Vietnam and Thailand are building out enterprise and cloud capacity simultaneously against grid infrastructure that was designed for industrial, not digital, loads. The Philippines is the outlier — a fragmented grid and typhoon risk profile that adds engineering complexity that the other five markets do not face.

The ASEAN Data Center Standards Report 2026 covers all six in a single document: market size by country, the standards compliance landscape (TIA-942, EN 50600, and local AHJ requirements), power and sustainability mandates, cooling constraints specific to tropical ambient conditions, and investment forecasts through 2030.

The cooling section deserves a specific note. Most data center design guidance is written against ASHRAE A2 or A3 thermal envelope assumptions that reflect temperate climates. The wet-bulb temperatures encountered in equatorial and monsoon-affected ASEAN sites — particularly in Manila, Jakarta, and Kuala Lumpur — place constraints on air-side economization that make direct comparisons with European or North American PUE benchmarks misleading. A facility achieving PUE 1.4 in Singapore is operating under a different thermodynamic constraint than a facility claiming the same number in Amsterdam.

The sustainability section covers the regulatory frameworks that are actually in force, not aspirational commitments. Singapore's Green Data Centre standard. Indonesia's RUPTL renewable targets and their delivery timeline. The absence of mandatory reporting requirements for embodied carbon in the Philippines and Vietnam.

The methodology uses public regulatory filings, operator announcements, and national grid authority publications as primary sources. Where numbers are projected rather than reported, the assumptions are stated.
