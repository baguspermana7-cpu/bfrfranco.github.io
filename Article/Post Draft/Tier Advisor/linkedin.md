Most data center operators can tell you their claimed tier. Fewer can tell you exactly which power, cooling, or site infrastructure subsystem is the binding constraint that determines that tier — and fewer still can map that constraint simultaneously against Uptime Institute, TIA-942, and EN 50600.

The three classification frameworks are not identical. A facility that achieves Uptime Tier III may not meet TIA-942 Rating 3 requirements in every domain. EN 50600 Availability Class 2 has overlap with Tier III but applies different verification criteria. Understanding all three is relevant if your compliance obligations involve multiple jurisdictions or customer contract requirements that reference different standards.

The Tier Advisor maps your infrastructure configuration against all three frameworks in a single assessment.

---

What the tool covers:

Infrastructure inputs across power, cooling, network, and site subsystems — the specific topology attributes that each classification framework uses to determine tier assignment.

The output maps to:
- Uptime Institute Tier I-IV with the availability percentage for each (99.671% to 99.9995%)
- TIA-942 Ratings 1-4
- EN 50600 Availability Classes

Regional compliance overlays for 12-plus jurisdictions show the regulatory baseline or recommendation for your geography.

The statistical analysis section includes subsystem availability modeling and the annualized downtime estimate for the assessed configuration.

Cost-to-upgrade estimates show the infrastructure delta and associated capital requirement for moving from the assessed tier to the next classification level.

---

The tool is particularly useful for two scenarios: validating a design against the tier classification it is intended to achieve before construction, and understanding the gap between current infrastructure and the next tier level when capacity upgrade discussions begin.
