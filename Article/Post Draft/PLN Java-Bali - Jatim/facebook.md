Something I find underappreciated about the Surabaya data center market: the grid resilience picture there is shaped by one very large fact — PLTU Paiton at 4.71 GW is the largest single coal block in Indonesia, and it feeds the East Java 500 kV ring that both the Surabaya metropolitan load and the Java-Bali submarine interconnect to Bali depend on.

A facility in Surabaya evaluating "independent" dual-feed redundancy needs to trace both paths up to the 500 kV level to understand whether they are actually independent upstream.

I mapped the full Jatim grid — Paiton and Gresik plant positions, the Surabaya DC cluster with operator feeder connections, petrochemical and cement industrial intakes, and the submarine interconnect landing — at resistancezero.com/pln-java-grid-jatim.html.

The industrial load composition in East Java — continuous-process petrochemical at Gresik and Tuban, cement at scale — also creates a different load profile from Jakarta that affects how dispatch events propagate through the provincial grid.
