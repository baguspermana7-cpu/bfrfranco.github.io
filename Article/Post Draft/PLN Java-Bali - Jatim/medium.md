Title: East Java's Grid — Why Paiton's 4.71 GW Matters Beyond the Province

---

The PLTU Paiton complex on the Probolinggo coast is not just East Java's generation anchor. At 4.71 GW, it is the single largest coal-fired block in Indonesia, and its position on the eastern 500 kV ring makes it structurally significant for the entire Java-Bali system.

To understand why, you need to see it in context with two other facts: PLTGU Gresik at 1.58 GW provides the gas-fired capacity that anchors the Surabaya metropolitan load, and the Java-Bali submarine interconnect — the physical cable that ties the two islands into a single synchronous machine — lands in East Java. The stability of the Bali grid is, in a physical sense, downstream of Paiton's dispatch state and the East Java 500 kV ring's integrity.

The Jawa Timur provincial grid detail page maps this structure with three overlays beyond the standard 500/150 kV backbone. The Surabaya DC cluster shows named operators with their 20 kV feeder connections — a geography that is denser than it appears in province-level grid maps because the Surabaya metropolitan area has attracted significant DC investment alongside the established industrial base. The industrial intakes layer covers the petrochemical corridor at Gresik and Tuban, cement producers, and coal terminal facilities — a set of continuous-process loads that shape the provincial load profile in ways that differ substantially from the Jakarta corridor.

The data is from PLN UID Distribusi Jawa Timur 2024 baseline and the overlay source file for operator positions. The submarine interconnect position is mapped from OSM infrastructure data.

For grid-resilience analysis at a Surabaya-area data center, the question of independence between primary and backup feeds has a concrete answer in the 500 kV topology: both paths from the eastern ring trace back to substations that are downstream of the Paiton dispatch block. That is a common-cause exposure that individual facility connection diagrams do not show.
