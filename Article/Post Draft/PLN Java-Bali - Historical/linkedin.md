Indonesia's energy transition narrative and its actual generation mix are two different stories. The Historical Energy Dashboard for the Java-Bali grid puts 10 years of generation data side by side so you can read the gap yourself.

The dashboard presents annual generation stacked by fuel type — coal, gas, hydro, geothermal, renewables — with a total demand overlay. It includes annual summary statistics, records for peak demand and minimum renewable share, and a month-by-month demand comparison for 2024 versus 2023.

The data sources are PLN Annual Report 2024, RUPTL 2025-2034, BPS Statistical Indonesia 2024, and IEA Indonesia 2024. These are representative figures, not real-time telemetry, but they are the most authoritative public baseline available for the Jamali system.

---

What the 10-year view makes visible:

The coal base in Java-Bali is not declining. It is large, established, and running at high utilization. Geothermal has grown steadily — Wayang Windu, Salak, Drajat, Patuha, and Dieng are material contributors to the Jawa Barat and Jateng renewable share. Hydro is constrained by reservoir capacity and seasonal variation, not investment.

The demand growth line matters for data center infrastructure planning: the Java-Bali grid has been absorbing significant load growth, and the DC sector is a visible contributor in the RUPTL projections.

---

If you are sizing backup power, evaluating grid-stability risk, or modeling emissions exposure for a Scope 2 calculation in Indonesia, the 10-year generation mix is the baseline you need to work from.
