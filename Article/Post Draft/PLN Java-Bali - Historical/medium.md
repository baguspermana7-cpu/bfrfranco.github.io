Title: 10 Years of Java-Bali Power Data — What the Generation Mix Actually Shows

---

Indonesia's energy transition receives consistent coverage in regional infrastructure reporting. The narrative is broadly optimistic: renewable capacity is growing, coal is on a managed phase-down path, and the country's RUPTL planning documents project an accelerating shift in generation mix through 2034.

The Historical Energy Dashboard for the Java-Bali grid is an attempt to hold that narrative against a decade of actual generation figures.

The data is sourced from PLN Annual Report 2024, RUPTL 2025-2034, BPS Statistical Indonesia 2024, and IEA Indonesia 2024. These are the most authoritative public figures available for the Jamali system. They are not real-time telemetry — the dashboard presents static representative figures for each year — but they are grounded in PLN's own disclosures rather than third-party estimates.

What the 10-year stacked view shows is the structural weight of coal in the Java-Bali generation mix. The absolute TWh output from coal-fired capacity has not declined over the observation period. Demand growth has absorbed new renewable additions without reducing thermal dispatch. The renewable share has grown as a percentage largely because total generation grew, not because thermal output fell.

The records section of the dashboard documents the peak demand years and the years with the highest and lowest renewable share — numbers that are hard to find in a single place from PLN's published reports.

For data center operators doing Scope 2 carbon accounting, the grid emission factor for Java-Bali is not a theoretical number. It reflects a generation mix that is coal-heavy and likely to remain so through the near-term planning horizon, regardless of renewable capacity additions.

The 2024 versus 2023 monthly demand comparison shows the load growth trajectory that the RUPTL baseline projects forward — and that the data center sector's contribution to new load is not a rounding error in the provincial figures.

Understanding the historical generation mix is the starting point for any credible grid-resilience or emissions analysis for a Java-based facility.
