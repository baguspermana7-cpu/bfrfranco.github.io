Most data center spare-parts programs are consumption-driven. You stocked what failed last time. That works fine until you get a fault mode you have never seen before — which is exactly how critical infrastructure fails.

The programs I have audited across hyperscale and colo sites share three gaps: no formal FMECA-to-stock linkage, no last-time-buy model for approaching EOL equipment, and no quantitative supplier risk index that updates when a vendor misses lead-time commitments.

I built the Critical Spares Engine to close those gaps in a single browser-based work environment.

---

What the engine covers across 27 modules:

Module group 1 — Operating Engine (OE-A through OE-P):
Daily PM operating system, supplier scorecard, negotiation strategy, contract checklist, process improvement, meeting intelligence, stakeholder planner, EOL response plan, ambiguity solver, interview/STAR builder, plus fleet portfolio and supply-chain transport layers.

Module group 2 — Quantitative Analysis:
- FMECA criticality scoring (severity x occurrence x detectability)
- METRIC and MEIO hub-positioning for multi-echelon inventory placement
- Kraljic matrix segmentation for strategic vs. routine procurement
- DMSMS risk scoring for diminishing manufacturing sources
- Monte Carlo simulation for stockout probability across lead-time scenarios
- Supplier risk index (SRI) with delivery-performance and financial-health inputs
- Last-time-buy economics with NPV modeling

Module group 3 — Supply Chain and Transport:
Lane and mode planner, supply-chain risk map, disruption scenario simulation, logistics cost and expedite calculator with 16 country-risk profiles.
