Jawa Tengah has the highest renewable share on Java at approximately 17%, and it also has Tanjung Jati B — one of the largest coal plants in Southeast Asia at 2.64 GW on the north coast. Both facts are true at the same time.

The renewable contribution comes from Mrica hydro on the Serayu cascade and the Dieng geothermal complex in the central highlands. The coal baseline also includes Cilacap at 1.4 GW on the south coast. The province is carrying a lot of generation capacity for a relatively measured peak load.

I mapped the full Jateng + DIY grid — 500/150 kV backbone, DC operators, industrial intakes, and the 20 kV overlay — at resistancezero.com/pln-java-grid-jateng.html.

There is also a carbon accounting angle here: Tanjung Jati B has been co-firing coal with biomass under Indonesia's mandate, which affects the effective emission factor for electricity dispatched from north Java substations.
