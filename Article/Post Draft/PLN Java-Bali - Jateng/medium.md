Title: Central Java's Grid Paradox — Highest Renewable Share, Heaviest Coal Corridor

---

Jawa Tengah and DIY hold a pair of facts that look contradictory until you see the map.

The province hosts Tanjung Jati B on its north coast at 2.64 GW — one of the largest coal-fired power stations in Southeast Asia. It also carries the highest renewable percentage share on the Java backbone at approximately 17%, above both Jabar and Jatim.

The explanation is Mrica and Dieng. The Mrica hydroelectric cascade on the Serayu River runs at significant installed capacity. The Dieng geothermal complex sits at altitude in the central highlands and contributes steady baseload renewable generation. Together they lift the renewable fraction above what any other central province achieves — while Tanjung Jati B continues to dominate total generation volume.

The carbon accounting implication is real. Facilities in the central Java corridor that use the Java-Bali system average emission factor are applying a figure that does not reflect local dispatch. Whether that works in their favor depends on which substation they are drawing from: substations downstream of Tanjung Jati B versus those supplied from the Dieng evacuation corridor see materially different dispatch mixes.

The Jawa Tengah + DIY provincial grid detail page maps the full 500/150 kV backbone for the province with 20 kV industrial-intake overlays — DC operators, cement producers, textile mills, and the evacuation routes from each major plant. The source data is PLN UID Jateng + DIY 2024 baseline.

A point on Tanjung Jati B worth noting for Scope 2 analysts: the plant has been co-firing coal with biomass under Indonesia's co-firing mandate, which changes the effective emission factor for dispatched electricity in a way that neither the system average nor a straightforward coal-plant factor captures. The precise co-firing ratio is not in the public dataset, but it is a variable that matters for any claim of partial renewable sourcing from north Java substations.
