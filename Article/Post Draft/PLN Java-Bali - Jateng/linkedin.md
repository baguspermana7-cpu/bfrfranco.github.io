Jawa Tengah and DIY present one of the more structurally interesting sections of the Java-Bali transmission backbone — an industrial generation corridor with both the heaviest coal concentration north of Solo and the highest renewable share percentage in Java.

That is not a contradiction. The renewable share figure reflects Mrica hydro on the Serayu cascade and the Dieng geothermal complex, which together lift the mix to approximately 17%. The coal baseline is Tanjung Jati B at 2.64 GW on the north coast and Cilacap at 1.4 GW on the south — two of the largest thermal plants on the island.

The note on Tanjung Jati B coal/biomass co-firing is relevant for carbon accounting: the plant's CLF (coal-to-biomass firing ratio) affects the effective emission factor for electricity dispatched from north Java substations in a way that system-average figures do not capture.
