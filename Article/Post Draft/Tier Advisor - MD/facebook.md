A question I get fairly often from people outside the industry: what does a tier number actually mean for a data center?

Here is the concrete version: Tier I means roughly 28 hours of potential downtime per year (99.671% availability). Tier IV means roughly 26 minutes per year (99.9995%). The jump from Tier III to Tier IV — which requires fully fault-tolerant, concurrently maintainable infrastructure — is where the capital cost increase becomes significant.

The more complicated reality for people operating or designing facilities is that there are three different frameworks that classify this: Uptime Institute Tiers, TIA-942 Ratings, and EN 50600 Availability Classes. They overlap but are not identical. Some customer contracts and regulatory requirements reference different frameworks, and a facility that meets one may not meet the equivalent level on another.

The Tier Advisor tool maps a single infrastructure configuration — power, cooling, network, site — against all three frameworks simultaneously. It also includes regional compliance overlays for 12-plus jurisdictions and cost-to-upgrade estimates for moving to the next tier.
