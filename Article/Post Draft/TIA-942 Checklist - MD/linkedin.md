A data center can claim Tier III compliance and still fail 12 of the 80+ TIA-942-B items that define what Tier III actually means. Most of those failures are not in the glamorous subsystems — they are in documentation, labeling, cable management segregation, and fire detection zone definition.

The gap between claimed tier level and demonstrated TIA-942-B compliance is larger than most operators would like to admit, and it rarely surfaces until a formal audit or an incident creates the documentation demand.

The TIA-942 Compliance Checklist was built to give teams a structured self-assessment framework before they need it under pressure.

---

What the checklist covers:

80-plus items across the six ANSI/TIA-942-B domains:
- Site and architecture (physical security perimeter, access control, structural ratings)
- Electrical (redundancy topology, UPS configuration, generator capacity and transfer time)
- Mechanical and cooling (N/N+1/2N topology, maintenance bypass, temperature monitoring)
- Telecommunications infrastructure (pathway separation, cable tray segregation, meet-me room)
- Fire protection (detection zones, suppression system certification, FM 200/CO2 agent compliance)
- Security (CCTV coverage, access logging, mantrap configuration)

Scoring applies weighted importance multipliers: electrical items carry 1.5x weight because electrical failures are the leading cause of data center downtime in Uptime Institute's annual global survey. A missing maintenance bypass on a critical cooling unit scores differently than an unlabeled patch panel.

Tier 1 through Tier 4 filtering applies the relevant requirements for the tier you are assessing against. Facility-type filtering adjusts the checklist for enterprise, colocation, hyperscale, edge, or modular configurations.

Pro features include gap analysis with prioritized remediation sequence, Monte Carlo risk simulation, multi-tier radar comparison, and remediation roadmap with cost and timeline estimates.
