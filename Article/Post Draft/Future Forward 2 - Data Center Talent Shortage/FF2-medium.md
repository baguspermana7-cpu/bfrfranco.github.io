## Is the data center talent shortage real?

Uptime Institute's 2025 survey counted approximately 340,000 unfilled data center positions globally, with 65% of operators reporting significant difficulty hiring or retaining qualified staff and roughly half unable to find qualified candidates at all. The industry frames this as a supply problem. The article argues supply is only part of it.

The 340,000 figure represents roles that companies listed, tried to fill and left open. But "unfilled" means no candidates meeting the posted requirements at the posted salary, which is structurally a different problem from no available candidates. The average operations posting lists between 12 and 18 distinct requirements, and companies reject 85-90% of applicants as underqualified, then cite high vacancy rates as evidence of a shortage. Pay compounds it: the gap between mid-tier data centers and cloud or AI companies is 25-40% for equivalent skills, and salary compression explains 80% of turnover gaps between hyperscalers and colo operators. Industry turnover has risen from 12% in 2020 to about 21% in 2025.

Regional pictures differ across 12 markets. Southeast Asia combines 35%+ CAGR in data center growth with the least mature training pipeline of any major market; Indonesia alone needs approximately 15,000 new professionals by 2028.

Two forces make the next decade harder. IEEE Spectrum and The Pragmatic Engineer documented a 25-60% collapse in entry-level engineering hiring between 2024 and 2025, so closing entry points now manufactures a senior shortage later. And approximately 40% of the facilities engineering community is over 50, with 50,000-70,000 experienced operators projected to leave between 2026 and 2029, taking institutional memory with them. Uptime attributes roughly 40% of outages to human error.
