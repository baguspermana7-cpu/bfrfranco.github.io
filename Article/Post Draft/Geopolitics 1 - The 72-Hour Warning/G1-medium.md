## Why 20+ nations want you ready for 72 hours

More than 20 nations now urge their citizens to prepare for at least 72 hours of self-sufficiency. The message is consistent across Europe, East Asia and the Americas, though the details vary: Sweden asks for a week, Finland a minimum of 72 hours, Germany 10 days, Taiwan a week and Japan two weeks of shelter capacity. Poland made 72 hours a legal mandate in its 2024 civil defense law.

The article traces why governments are acting now. The Russian invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 broke the post-Cold War European security order. Nord Stream was sabotaged in September 2022, and Baltic pipelines and cables were damaged from 2023 onward. NEW START expired on February 5, 2026, so for the first time since 1972 there are no limits on US and Russian nuclear arsenals. Military planners now acknowledge that conflicts may begin with infrastructure disruption, cyber attacks and gray-zone activity rather than obvious military action.

The shelter data shows how unevenly states have prepared. Sweden has about 65,000 shelters with 7 million capacity and Finland more than 50,000 with 4.5 million; South Korea has 17,000+ public shelters. Germany has 579 functional shelters for 83 million people, down from 2,000+ at the Cold War peak, which leaves 99%+ of citizens without access. Indonesia and the wider ASEAN region have no civil defense shelter network and focus on natural disasters only.

The practical guidance is modest: at least 3 liters of drinking water per person per day, non-perishable food needing minimal preparation, a battery or hand-crank radio, cash because ATMs may not work, and a 2-week supply of prescription medication. The article's conclusion is that preparedness is not panic: governments are not predicting imminent catastrophe, they are acknowledging a changed risk environment.
