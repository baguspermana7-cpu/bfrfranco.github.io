Model families in 2026: who is how big

As of mid-October 2026 the models worth knowing fall into two groups. Open-weight models publish their size, so their memory footprint can be computed. Closed models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google publish a context window and a price but no parameter count, and any figure quoted for them is a guess. Among the open models, the largest are mixtures of experts with one to two orders of magnitude fewer active than total parameters.

7.1 Dense versus MoE, open versus closed

A dense model uses all its parameters on every token, so total equals active. An MoE has a total much larger than its active count (section 5). Memory, and therefore the hardware tier, follows the total; per-token compute follows the active count. For closed models, parameter counts are not published by Anthropic, OpenAI or Google, and this article does not repeat figures seen elsewhere.

7.3 Tokens per parameter: why small models are over-trained

Divide pre-training tokens by parameters and the families separate sharply. The compute-optimal ratio from the Chinchilla study is about 20 [S5]. Everything else in the table follows from a different goal: a model that will be served billions of times is cheaper to run if it is small, so extra training compute is spent once to buy cheaper inference for its whole life.

Operator takeaway

A model name tells you less than three numbers: total parameters for the memory tier, active parameters for compute per token, and the licence for whether you may host it. For closed models you get a price and a window, and the hardware is the vendor’s concern.
