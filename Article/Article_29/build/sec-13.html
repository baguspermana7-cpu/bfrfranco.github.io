The hardware ladder, rung by rung

The same model runs on a laptop, a workstation card, a server, a liquid-cooled rack or a campus of tens of thousands of accelerators. What changes from rung to rung is memory, memory bandwidth and the power and cooling needed to feed them. The model that fits a rung is set by its weight memory plus its KV cache, and the speed is set by bandwidth. For a facility engineer the important step is the one from server to rack, where air cooling stops being an option.

The serving rule used throughout: memory needed = weights (N × bytes per parameter) + KV cache (see the KV-cache section and its calculator) + roughly 10–20% runtime overhead (a rule of thumb, not a sourced figure). Training needs about 16 B per parameter plus activations [S60]; the energy calculator in the training section turns a GPU count into megawatts.

Laptop and Mac Studio

Apple's unified memory lets a laptop-class chip hold a model that would need a data-center GPU elsewhere. The M4 Max offers up to 128 GB at 546 GB/s, and the Mac Studio M3 Ultra up to 512 GB at 819 GB/s [S71]. A 70B model at Q4_K_M (43 GB) fits an M4 Max. DeepSeek-V3 at about 4 bits needs 671 × 0.5 = 336 GB to 671 × 4.894 / 8 = 410 GB and so fits the 512 GB M3 Ultra (derived). The bandwidth ceilings from the serving section apply: about 19 tok/s for 70B Q4 on the M3 Ultra and 13 on the M4 Max. Newer Apple silicon than these two parts was not checked for this article.

Workstation GPU

An RTX 5090 carries 32 GB of GDDR7 at 1,792 GB/s, draws 575 W and asks for a 1,000 W power supply [S15]. An RTX 4090 has 24 GB (its 1,008 GB/s figure is from secondary sources). These cards serve an 8B model at BF16, a 14B at 8-bit or a 32B at 4-bit (about 20 GB, derived), and fine-tune 8–14B models with QLoRA.

One server

One rack: NVL72

The GB200 NVL72 joins 72 GPUs and 36 Grace CPUs in a single NVLink domain with 13.4 TB of HBM3E, 576 TB/s of aggregate memory bandwidth and 130 TB/s of NVLink bandwidth, and it is liquid-cooled [S10]. The GB300 NVL72 raises memory to 20 TB and gives each GPU an 800 Gb/s network interface [S11]. HPE lists the GB200 NVL72 rack at 132 kW nominal TDP and about 192 kW peak electrical design power [S68]. Per GPU that is 132 / 72 = 1.83 kW (derived).

What fits: a trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with long context, since Kimi K2 at FP8 is about 1 TB (derived) against 13.4 TB of HBM, and a full fine-tune of a 70B model (1.13 TB). NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL144 is reported for the second half of 2026 at 288 GB HBM4 per GPU and 120–130 kW per rack, but those figures come from secondary reporting of a roadmap and are unverified [S75].

A pod

Beyond one NVLink domain, racks are joined by scale-out networks: InfiniBand or RoCE at 400–800 Gb/s per GPU [S2][S11]. Llama 3 405B was trained on 16,384 H100 over such a fabric [S2]. Google's answer is the TPU pod: an Ironwood pod joins 9,216 chips for 42.5 EFLOPS of FP8, draws about 10 MW and is liquid-cooled; one chip has 192 GB of HBM at 7.37 TB/s and 4,614 TFLOPS FP8 [S14]. All-in that is 10 MW / 9,216 = 1.09 kW per chip (derived). Vertiv and NVIDIA publish a 7 MW reference architecture for GB200 NVL72 [S68].

A campus

xAI states that Colossus was built in 122 days and then doubled in 92 days to 200,000 GPUs [S19]; the site's article 23 covers that build. OpenAI states that Stargate Abilene uses GB200 systems and that GPT-5.5 was trained there [S21]; Epoch estimates the site reaches its planned 1.2 GW in the fourth quarter of 2026 [S20]. Claims about Colossus beyond 200,000 GPUs rest on secondary sources and are not used here. Cluster power scales linearly from the unit figures (derived, PUE of 1.2 assumed):

  • 100,000 H100 × 1.275 kW = 127.5 MW IT; × 1.2 = 153 MW, in line with the roughly 150 MW public figure for Colossus phase 1 in article 23.
  • 100,000 GB200 = 100,000 / 72 = 1,389 racks × 132 kW = 183 MW IT; × 1.2 = 220 MW. Epoch's estimate for GPT-6 Astra is about 233 MW [S65].

Inference ASICs: Groq and Cerebras

These chips trade capacity for bandwidth by keeping weights in on-chip SRAM. The Groq chip holds 230 MB of SRAM at 80 TB/s, draws 215 W TDP and is scheduled deterministically by its compiler [S17]. At 230 MB per chip a 70B FP8 model needs 70.6 GB / 230 MB = 307 chips for the weights alone (derived), so deployments are many-chip racks. The Cerebras WSE-3 puts 44 GB of SRAM at 21 PB/s and 900,000 cores on one 46,225 mm² wafer; the CS-3 system draws about 27 kW, occupies 16 RU and is liquid-cooled. Its datasheet figure of 125 PFLOPS FP16 is a sparse number, while the company's current web page claims 250 PFLOPS for a “WSE-3 Turbo” without stating the basis [S16].

What it means for your facility

  • Density. A single DGX H100 at 10.2 kW already sits at or above what a conventional rack is commonly provisioned for (a rule of thumb of 4–8 kW, not sourced here). An NVL72 at 132 kW is more than ten times that eight-GPU node (132 / 10.2 = 12.9).
  • Liquid cooling. GB200 and GB300 NVL72 and Ironwood are liquid-cooled [S10][S11][S14]. Vertiv's reference design supports up to 132 kW per rack with hybrid liquid and air [S68]. Google says direct liquid cooling can “reliably sustain up to twice the performance of standard air cooling” [S14].
  • Power quality. Synchronous training makes cluster power swing “on the order of tens of megawatts”, and Llama 3's 16K-GPU run logged 419 unexpected interruptions in 54 days [S2]. UPS, generator and utility interconnection need to be designed for that load step.
  • PUE. The campus figures above assume a PUE of 1.2; substitute your own with the site's PUE calculator.

The rack is the design unit

From NVL72 upward, the 72 GPUs, the NVLink fabric and the cold plates are one product. Plan floor loading, coolant distribution and electrical feeds per rack at 132 kW nominal and 192 kW peak design, and treat the training load step as part of the electrical specification, not as an operations surprise.
