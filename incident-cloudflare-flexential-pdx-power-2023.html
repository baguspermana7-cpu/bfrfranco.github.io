Cloudflare Control-Plane & Analytics Outage — Flexential PDX-04 Power Failure (Nov 2023)

Cloudflare 2023-11-02 40h 41m core impact PowerHuman error

On 2 November 2023 a Portland General Electric (PGE) unplanned maintenance event dropped one of two independent utility feeds into Flexential's PDX-04 data center near Hillsboro, Oregon. Roughly three hours later a ground fault on a 12,470-volt PGE transformer triggered protective systems that, by design, shut down fast — but the same protective trip also shut down all ten of PDX-04's generators, which were unusually running in parallel with the surviving utility feed. UPS batteries meant to bridge to generator power began failing after only about four minutes, and the entire facility went dark. Cloudflare's control plane and analytics ran primarily across three Hillsboro-area data centers on an assumption that any one could fail, but two critical log-processing services (Kafka and ClickHouse) existed only in PDX-04 and had dependents in the supposedly highly-available cluster; PDX-04 also held more than a third of the HA cluster and Cloudflare's largest analytics cluster. That hidden single-site dependency — a failure mode Cloudflare had never tested by fully taking PDX-04 offline — broke the redundancy design. Cloudflare's non-automated failover to European disaster-recovery sites was slow, and cold-start recovery of servers and services ran into 4 November.

Root cause

The November 2, 2023 Cloudflare control-plane and analytics outage originated not inside Cloudflare's network but in the electrical infrastructure of the Flexential PDX-04 colocation facility near Hillsboro, Oregon — one of three Oregon data centers on which Cloudflare's control plane and analytics run active-active. PDX-04 was the largest of the three, housing Cloudflare's largest analytics cluster and more than a third of its high-availability-cluster machines. The trigger was utility-side: at 08:50 UTC, Portland General Electric (PGE) had an unplanned maintenance event that took down one of the facility's power feeds. This should have been survivable — the facility has multiple feeds with some level of independence, plus ten on-site generators (inclusive of redundant units) rated to carry full load. Instead a sequence of atypical operational decisions and hidden dependencies produced a full facility power loss. Flexential failed the facility over to generator power but, counter to best practice, did not notify Cloudflare, so Cloudflare had no early warning the site was degraded. More consequentially, Flexential ran the remaining utility feed and the generators simultaneously rather than isolating onto generators — an arrangement Cloudflare called 'unusual.' Cloudflare notes Flexential participated in a PGE program called DSG (Dispatchable Standby Generation), under which the utility can run a data center's generators to help supply the grid and, in exchange, helps maintain the generators and supplies fuel; Cloudflare could find no record of ever being informed of this participation. The precise mechanism coupling the running utility feed and generators is therefore inferred by Cloudflare, not confirmed by any official Flexential or PGE root-cause document. At approximately 11:40 UTC a ground fault developed on a PGE transformer at PDX-04. Because the utility feed and generators were running together, the protective trip did not merely isolate the utility side — it shut down all ten generators. Both primary generation sources were now offline together, and the facility fell back on its UPS battery banks, designed only to bridge the brief gap for a generator restart. The batteries, supposedly sufficient for roughly ten minutes, started to fail after only about four minutes based on Cloudflare's observation of its own equipment; there is no confirmed official explanation from Flexential or PGE for the shortfall. Cloudflare's two routers connecting PDX-04 to the outside world went offline at 11:44 UTC (Cloudflare's first indication of a problem), and complete facility power loss followed around 12:00 UTC. Recovery was slowed by facility-side conditions: generators required a manual physical restart; electronic access-control/badge systems were down because they too depended on facility power; the overnight shift was only security plus a single unaccompanied technician who had been on the job for a week; and when Flexential attempted to re-power Cloudflare's circuits the circuit breakers were found to be faulty (cause unclear) and had to be replaced. Flexential first acknowledged the issue to Cloudflare at 12:28 UTC and restarted generators at 12:48 UTC. Finally, the blast radius was widened by a latent flaw in Cloudflare's own design. PDX-04 was meant to be one of three redundant facilities such that any single-site loss would be transparent, but two critical services — Kafka and ClickHouse, which process logs and power analytics — ran only in PDX-04 while high-availability-cluster services depended on them. Cloudflare had never tested fully taking the entire PDX-04 facility offline, so these hard dependencies went undiscovered and failover was not clean. Cloudflare decided to fail over to disaster recovery at 13:40 UTC, brought first services up on DR at 13:43 UTC, and reached DR stability at 17:57 UTC. Clean utility power was confirmed at 22:48 UTC on November 2, but full restoration was not completed until 04:25 UTC on November 4. In short, an ordinary utility maintenance event escalated into total facility power loss via an atypical generator/utility co-running configuration, a transformer ground fault that tripped all generators, a UPS bank that failed far short of its rating, faulty breakers, and slow manual recovery — while Cloudflare's control plane and analytics failed to ride through it because untested single-facility dependencies defeated the intended three-site redundancy.

Contributing factors

  • Atypical facility configuration: Flexential ran the remaining utility feed and the on-site generators simultaneously rather than isolating onto generator power. Cloudflare called this 'unusual.' The coupling meant a single PGE transformer ground fault could — and did — trip the entire ten-unit generator fleet, defeating the purpose of independent backup generation.
  • Undisclosed DSG participation: Flexential was part of PGE's DSG (Dispatchable Standby Generation) program, under which the utility can run the facility's generators to support the grid and in exchange helps maintain them and supplies fuel. Cloudflare could find no record of ever being informed, hiding a latent risk in how the facility's power was operated. (Cloudflare does not spell out the acronym in its post-mortem; it presents the DSG link as its inferred explanation for the co-running configuration.)
  • Communication / notification failure: Counter to best practice, Flexential did not tell Cloudflare it had failed over to generator power at 08:50 UTC, so Cloudflare had no early warning that PDX-04 was in a degraded, single-point-of-failure state. The first Flexential acknowledgment did not arrive until 12:28 UTC — after the collapse.
  • UPS batteries failed far below rated capacity: The banks meant to bridge the gap, supposedly good for ~10 minutes, started failing after only about 4 minutes (per Cloudflare's observation of its own equipment losing power), removing the last automated safeguard almost immediately and leaving no time to intervene. No confirmed official explanation for the shortfall exists.
  • Recovery obstacles — thin staffing, dependent systems, and faulty breakers: Generators needed a manual physical restart; electronic access-control/badge systems were down because they also depended on facility power; the overnight shift was only security plus one unaccompanied technician on the job a week; and when power-up was attempted the circuit breakers were discovered faulty and needed replacement — all of which delayed restoration.
  • Untested single-facility dependencies in Cloudflare's own HA design: Two critical services, Kafka and ClickHouse (log processing and analytics), ran only in PDX-04 while high-availability-cluster services depended on them. Cloudflare had never tested fully taking PDX-04 offline, so these hard dependencies went undiscovered and the control plane and analytics could not cleanly fail over — turning a single-site power loss into a multi-day outage.

Correction of errors (COE)

  • Ensure the control plane running ON Cloudflare's network continues to function even if all core data centers are offline (push control-plane serving to the edge/network so it survives total core loss). Cloudflare · Committed
  • Remove products' hard dependency on the availability of any single core data center — mandate that HA-cluster services (and the products depending on them) actually tolerate a full single-site loss, including relocating single-site services like Kafka and ClickHouse. Cloudflare · Committed
  • Test the blast radius of system failures and minimize the number of services impacted by any one failure; adopt more rigorous chaos-style testing including fully taking an entire facility (e.g. PDX-04) offline, a scenario never previously exercised. Cloudflare · Committed
  • Establish and regularly test true disaster-recovery capability for the control plane and analytics so failover is fast and automated rather than the slow manual process seen here (DR not stable until ~4h14m after first turn-up). Cloudflare · Committed
  • Provide a logging/analytics disaster-recovery plan so raw logs and Logpush are not lost during a single-site outage (raw logs were down the full incident; some logs were dropped unrecoverably). Cloudflare · Committed
  • Thoroughly audit all core data centers and their power/redundancy assumptions, and reassess reliance on facilities that can lose both utility and generator power via a common protective trip. Cloudflare · Committed
  • Facility-side: stop running the remaining single utility feed and generators in parallel during a degraded state, and notify tenants immediately upon failover to generator power (best-practice communications Flexential did not follow). Inferred corrective expectation, not a Cloudflare-stated commitment. Flexential (facility operator) · Corrective expectation raised by Cloudflare

Lessons learnt

  • 'Redundant on paper' is not redundant if any critical service is single-homed: Cloudflare's three-site 'any-one-can-fail' assumption was silently invalidated by Kafka and ClickHouse living only in PDX-04 and by >1/3 of the HA cluster residing there. Redundancy claims must be enforced by dependency mapping, not assumed.
  • Untested failure modes are latent outages: because Cloudflare had never exercised a full PDX-04 blackout, the single-site dependencies stayed hidden until a real, worst-case event surfaced them all at once. Game-day / chaos testing that actually kills a whole facility is the only way to prove blast radius.
  • Facility power redundancy can share a common failure: ten full-load-rated generators provided no protection when one high-voltage ground fault's protective trip took them all down together — especially while generators were paralleled with the utility. Diverse feeds and generator counts do not guarantee independence if a single protection scheme can shed everything.
  • UPS bridge time is a safety-critical assumption that must be verified, not trusted: batteries failing after ~4 minutes (versus their intended ~10-minute bridge) turned a survivable generator-restart gap into a total blackout. Battery health and true runtime need continuous validation.
  • Cross-organization communication is part of the control loop: Flexential not announcing the generator failover, and taking ~44 minutes to send a first notice, left Cloudflare blind to a degraded single-point-of-power state it might otherwise have hedged against.
  • Disaster recovery must be fast and automated to be useful: a DR failover that takes hours to stabilize (13:43 → 17:57 UTC) and a cold start running into a second day mean the control plane's resilience was theoretical, not operational.

Improvements & remediation

  • Serve the control plane from Cloudflare's edge network so it keeps functioning even with all core data centers offline, eliminating the core-site single point of failure.
  • Enforce genuine multi-site availability for all HA-dependent services — relocate/replicate single-homed services (Kafka, ClickHouse) and forbid HA-cluster dependents from relying on a single facility.
  • Institutionalize blast-radius and chaos testing, including fully powering down an entire core facility, to surface hidden single-site dependencies before customers do.
  • Stand up and continuously exercise automated, fast disaster recovery for control plane, analytics, and the logging pipeline (so raw logs / Logpush survive a single-site loss).
  • Audit facility power designs and require operators to (a) avoid running a single utility feed paralleled with generators in a degraded state and (b) immediately notify Cloudflare on any failover to generator power; validate UPS battery health/runtime against spec.
  • Weight and distribute the three Hillsboro-area sites so no single site holds a disproportionate share (>1/3) of the HA cluster or uniquely hosts the largest analytics cluster.

Comprehensive analysis

Failure mechanism: a protection scheme that shed generation instead of the fault

The initiating electrical event was a ground fault on a 12,470-volt PGE transformer feeding PDX-04. High-voltage ground faults are designed to force a fast protective shutdown to prevent equipment damage, and that is what happened — but the protection acted on the wrong scope. Instead of isolating the faulted section and letting the facility ride through on its own generation, the trip took all ten of PDX-04's generators offline simultaneously. The critical aggravator was the operating mode: Flexential was running the one surviving utility feed and the generators in parallel, an unusual configuration that gave the fault a path into the paralleled generation. With grid and generators lost together, the entire electrical resilience of a site rated for full-load generator support collapsed to whatever the UPS could hold.

Why a survivable gap became a blackout, then a cascade

The UPS existed precisely to bridge the seconds-to-minutes gap of a generator restart. It failed that single job: batteries began failing after roughly four minutes, well short of their intended ~10-minute bridge, so between 11:44 and 12:01 UTC PDX-04 went completely dark before generation was restored. The blackout then met a software architecture that assumed the opposite. Cloudflare's control plane and analytics ran primarily across three Hillsboro-area sites on the premise that losing any one was tolerable — but PDX-04 uniquely hosted Kafka and ClickHouse (the log-processing backbone) whose dependents ran in the high-availability cluster, and PDX-04 held more than a third of that cluster and the largest analytics cluster. Losing the single facility therefore didn't degrade the system gracefully; it broke services that were explicitly supposed to survive a one-site loss, taking down dashboard/API config changes, analytics, and raw logs.

Recovery: manual, serial, and slow

Recovery exposed how little of the resilience was automated. Flexential restarted generators at 12:48 UTC, but that only restored site power potential; Cloudflare still had to replace failed circuit breakers, rebuild servers, and fail the control plane over to European DR sites. The DR decision came at 13:40 UTC, first services at 13:43 UTC, but stability not until 17:57 UTC — over four hours of hand-driven failover. Clean utility power at PDX-04 was confirmed at 22:48 UTC on 2 November, yet the server and service rebuild dragged on until full restoration at 04:25 UTC on 4 November. The long tail — roughly 40 hours end to end — was dominated not by the power event itself but by the absence of fast, tested, automated recovery for a control plane that had quietly become single-site-dependent.

Organizational and systemic factors

Two organizations contributed. On the facility side, Flexential ran a risky degraded configuration (utility paralleled with generators), its UPS underperformed, its overnight shift lacked an experienced operations or electrical expert and its access-control system was not on battery backup, and — counter to best practice — it never told Cloudflare it had gone to generator power, with its first outage notice arriving about 44 minutes after impact. On the Cloudflare side, the deeper systemic root cause was that it had never tested fully taking PDX-04 offline, so the divergence between its stated redundancy model and its actual dependency graph was never surfaced. Redundancy was assumed rather than enforced and verified; the concentration of Kafka, ClickHouse, a third of the HA cluster, and the largest analytics cluster in one site was a latent single point of failure that only a real blackout revealed.

Cross-incident pattern: the redundancy-verification gap

This incident is a textbook instance of a recurring failure class in large distributed systems: correlated failure of nominally independent redundancy, compounded by unverified availability claims. The facility layer showed correlated power failure (one fault taking all generators and the UPS bridge with it, despite N+ generator counts), while the software layer showed a hidden single-homed dependency inside a 'highly available' cluster. The through-line — matching many high-profile cloud outages — is that resilience was designed but never proven by deliberately destroying a whole failure domain. Cloudflare's own remediation reflects the general lesson: push the control plane to the edge so it survives total core loss, mandate real multi-site availability, and continuously test blast radius by taking entire facilities down on purpose.

Technical deep-dive

The failure cascaded through four layers of the power-delivery and redundancy stack, each of which was supposed to arrest it and each of which was defeated. Layer 1 — Utility feeds. PDX-04 has multiple feeds with some level of independence (Cloudflare's careful wording — it does not claim full independence, nor state an exact count). At 08:50 UTC an unplanned PGE maintenance event removed one feed. One feed remaining should have been sufficient, so this was a survivable single-feed loss, yet Flexential initiated a transfer to generator power. Layer 2 — Generators. PDX-04 has ten generators (inclusive of redundant units) rated to carry full load. In a correct transfer the facility isolates load onto generators (or onto the surviving feed), keeping the sources electrically separated so a fault on one cannot propagate to the other. Instead Flexential ran the remaining utility feed and the generators at the same time — an 'unusual' arrangement Cloudflare tentatively attributes to PGE's DSG (Dispatchable Standby Generation) program. Because the fleet and the feed were coupled, when a ground fault developed on a PGE transformer at PDX-04 at ~11:40 UTC, the protective measure that responded shut down all ten generators rather than isolating only the faulted utility side. A ground fault is an unintended current path to earth; protective relays trip to prevent equipment damage and personnel hazard, but here the protection took the entire generation capability offline. The precise protection/interlock design is not confirmed by any official Flexential source. Both generation sources — utility and generators — were now dead together. Layer 3 — UPS batteries. The UPS banks carry load for the short bridge between loss of source power and restoration of generation, supposedly ~10 minutes here, and are the last automated safeguard. In this event they started to fail after only about four minutes (per Cloudflare's observation of its own gear losing power) and then depleted, with complete facility power loss around 12:00 UTC. Whether the shortfall owed to battery health, state of charge, load, or the abnormal preceding switching sequence is not established by any official Flexential/PGE root-cause report; the ~4-minute figure is Cloudflare's empirical observation. The practical effect was that a safeguard meant to buy ~10 minutes for a manual restart delivered under half that — Cloudflare's two edge routers connecting PDX-04 externally went dark at 11:44 UTC, its first indication of the problem. Layer 4 — Cloudflare application redundancy. Cloudflare's control plane and analytics run active-active across three Hillsboro-area data centers, continuously syncing so a single-site loss should be transparent. The defeating flaw was a hidden dependency graph: two critical services, Kafka and ClickHouse (log processing and analytics), ran only in PDX-04, while services on the nominally high-availability cluster depended on them. Cloudflare had never tested fully taking the entire PDX-04 facility offline, so this coupling went undiscovered; when PDX-04 lost power those dependencies became unavailable and failover was not clean. Recovery required a deliberate cutover to disaster-recovery sites (decided 13:40 UTC, first services up 13:43 UTC, stable 17:57 UTC), further slowed on the facility side by a manual generator restart, downed badge/access systems, thin overnight staffing, and circuit breakers found faulty during power-up. Generators restarted 12:48 UTC; clean utility power was confirmed 22:48 UTC on Nov 2; full restoration reached 04:25 UTC on Nov 4. The engineering lesson: redundancy is only as strong as its least-redundant hard dependency, and it is dependency mapping and full site-loss testing — not merely replica placement — that determine whether multi-site architecture actually survives a site loss.
