NIRS Daejeon Government Data Center: UPS Lithium-Ion Battery Fire During Relocation Work

National Information Resources Service (NIRS) 2025-09-26 restoration time not established FirePower
SummaryTimelineResponseFMEACausesActionsSources

Summary

On the evening of 26 September 2025, a lithium-ion UPS battery that had been disconnected in order to be relocated to the basement of the NIRS Daejeon government data center entered thermal runaway and exploded in a server room, igniting a fire that took roughly ten hours to extinguish. When the fire broke out, all 647 government IT systems at the Daejeon headquarters were taken offline (96 of them directly destroyed, the rest shut down as a precautionary safety measure). Core national services including digital IDs, Korea Post postal banking, emergency GPS tracking, courts and tax were paralyzed, and all documents on the G-Drive cloud used by up to 30 ministries and nearly 191,000 employees were permanently lost because the repository was not backed up externally. Recovery reached 70.5 percent by 24 October and later exceeded 95 percent; the NIRS chief and three other officials were booked for causing the fire through on-duty negligence, the chief was relieved of duty, and Daejeon police raided NIRS.

Nearly 191,000 civil servants (out of 750,000 public-sector officials nationwide) had access to the affected G-Drive cloud as of August 2025; nationwide citizen impact via digital IDs, postal banking, emergency GPS tracking, courts and tax systems

Failure mode & effects analysis (FMEA)

The NIRS Daejeon government data center during relocation of a lithium-ion UPS battery bank: the battery asset, its isolation and discharge before handling, the contractor chain doing the work, battery-level detection, suppression and firefighting in a server room, the shared power and cooling that set how many systems went down, the G-Drive repository that had no backup, and the restoration effort. The ignition trigger is officially undetermined ('for an unknown reason'); the negligence findings (auxiliary power still connected, cells at 80-90% charge, unqualified crew, illegal subcontracting) come from press reporting of police statements, not a published forensic report; and many technical specifics (battery make, 384 modules, 2014 install, June 2025 inspection, ~22 h full extinguishment, 858 TB) are single-source or press-derived and not re-verified at review. Every corrective action in the dossier is 'Proposed' or 'In progress', none stated as done or committed by NIRS, so no revised score is claimed: all eight modes are residual risk.

Root cause and contributing factors

At approximately 20:20 KST on 2025-09-26 a fire broke out in the fifth-floor UPS room of the National Information Resources Service (NIRS) headquarters in Yuseong-gu, Daejeon, during work to physically relocate lithium-ion UPS batteries.
Full root-cause narrative
Per the Korean-language record, a worker switched off the UPS lithium-ion battery power and, about 40 minutes later, sparks flew from a battery 'for an unknown reason' (미상의 이유로) and ignited the fire — so the exact ignition trigger was officially still undetermined and under forensic testing, even though the surrounding negligence was established. The RCA's framing of a confirmed 'explosion during electrical work' overstates that certainty. Korean National Police investigators concluded the relocation was performed on batteries that were neither electrically isolated nor made energetically safe to handle, and filed professional-negligence charges. Two conditions compounded. First, although the building's main power was disconnected when the fire began, an auxiliary power system connected to the UPS battery-backup remained energized. Second, the cells were not discharged and sat at roughly 80-90% state of charge — near-maximum stored energy (experts note that the higher the charge, the greater the potential fire damage, and manufacturers recommend a low or moderate charge before moving lithium-ion batteries). Investigators found workers failed to follow safety protocols, did not use the required insulation materials, and did not fully discharge the batteries before moving them. Human and procedural safeguards were absent: the staff were not qualified for the job and had no battery-relocation experience; the battery manufacturers were deliberately excluded to cut costs; and the work had been illegally subcontracted — the two firms that won the tender handed it to three other businesses in breach of Korean law, with the two primary contractors falsifying employment records to make the crews appear to be their own staff. Consequences were severe because recovery redundancy was inadequate at the data layer. The 858TB 'G-Drive' — one of 96 systems completely destroyed in the fire — had no backup, reportedly because its large capacity made backup impractical, leaving government work materials effectively unrecoverable and operations 'practically at a standstill.' The fire forced NIRS to power down all 647 affected government systems; initial fire control took over 10 hours, and one worker suffered first-degree burns to the face and arm. Five people were charged with professional negligence resulting in fire — one NIRS official, one project-management-firm worker, and three construction-company employees — with charges reported in late October 2025. Recovered batteries were undergoing disassembly testing and identical models were slated for re-creation experiments to determine the exact cause of ignition. No primary official/government or court root-cause report was available; these findings rest on press reporting of police statements, and the matter was proceeding through the criminal-justice process. Official confirmation. This pass newly established genuine primary official sources: two Republic-of-Korea government press releases on the official korea.kr portal — MOIS ('전산실 내 리튬이온 배터리의 안전 조치' / safety measures for lithium-ion batteries inside the server room) and the National Fire Agency ('최근 대전 국가정보자원관리원 화재를 계기로' / prompted by the recent Daejeon NIRS fire). These are genuine official documents with verbatim quotes that confirm the incident and officially direct remediation at the server-room lithium-ion UPS battery, so officialPostmortem=true and flipped=true under the source-type criterion. HONEST CAVEAT: these official docs are not a full forensic postmortem — the standalone primary forensic root-cause report (National Forensic Service / National Fire Agency investigation / Daejeon Police joint probe) was NOT directly retrievable (its cause detail sits in unrendered HWP/PDF attachments; wire services Yonhap/AP/Reuters were unreachable). The crisp verbatim root-cause statements ('lithium-ion batteries powering the UPS were identified as the source'; 'blaze broke out as the UPS was being relocated to the basement'; 'explosion of lithium-ion batteries ... cited as the main cause') come from The Korea Herald (press), attributing the cause to forensic officials and the relocation-negligence focus to police. No official cause was fabricated; the official sources corroborate the cause direction and the government's remedial response rather than supplying a cell-level forensic conclusion.

Contributing factors by category

  • Unqualified, inexperienced personnel performing the Li-ion relocation: police reportedly found the crew had no experience with large-scale battery relocations and deemed them unqualified (Anadolu, press-derived, not re-verified at review).
  • Illegal subcontracting of the relocation work reportedly broke the qualification/accountability chain (Anadolu, press-derived).
  • Safety protocol not followed during disconnection — plausibly disconnecting wiring before the pack was fully de-energised, producing an arc/voltage spike; ko.wikipedia (verified) labels the spark cause 'unknown,' and the arc hypothesis is secondary (breached.company).
  • Aged asset kept in service and physically relocated rather than retired: reported as an LG Energy Solution bank installed August 2014, ~11 years old and past ~10-year recommended life (breached.company, single-source secondary, unverified at review).
  • No battery backup for the ~858 TB G-Drive ('couldn't have a backup system due to its large capacity') — a disaster-recovery governance failure that converted a contained fire into permanent data loss for ~125,000 officials (Anadolu/DCD, press-derived).
  • Physical siting of a volatile Li-ion bank close to production servers with inadequate fire separation, exposing adjacent racks to runaway heat and smoke (breached.company, secondary).
  • Suppression regime mismatched to chemistry: server-hall water-cautious firefighting could not arrest a self-oxidising Li-ion runaway, driving the verified >10h initial knockdown (ko.wikipedia).
  • Loss of HVAC/CRAC (항온항습장치) forced a protective mass power-down of 647 systems, widening the outage well beyond the fire's footprint (ko.wikipedia, verified).
  • No disclosed battery-level off-gas/thermal-runaway early-warning detection; the event was human-noticed at ~20:15 rather than caught at cell venting (ko.wikipedia, verified).

Analysis

What happened (verified core)

On the evening of 2025-09-26, during relocation work on a UPS lithium-ion battery in the 5th-floor server/UPS room of the NIRS Daejeon government data center, a worker switched the battery power off and about 40 minutes later, at ~20:20 KST, sparks flew from the battery 'for an unknown reason' and a fire broke out (ko.wikipedia, verified verbatim). A first report came at ~20:15. NIRS powered off all 647 Daejeon systems as the room's HVAC/CRAC failed, and roughly 96 government online services were interrupted. Water suppression was constrained by fear of destroying the data-storage equipment, so initial knockdown took over 10 hours. One worker suffered 1st-degree burns.

Why it became a disaster (governance amplifiers)

The physical fire was contained to a room, but the impact was national. Press reporting (Anadolu/DCD, not re-verifiable at review) states the ~858 TB G-Drive store — data for ~125,000 officials — had no backup 'due to its large capacity,' making its loss permanent, while other destroyed systems were recoverable. Police reportedly charged five people (an NIRS official, a PM-firm employee, three construction workers) with professional negligence, citing an unqualified, inexperienced crew and illegal subcontracting. The disaster scale was manufactured by DR governance, contractor governance, and asset-lifecycle failures, not by the battery alone.

The Li-ion suppression conflict

Lithium-ion thermal runaway is self-oxidising, so clean-agent and water-cautious server-hall firefighting cannot arrest it, and it propagates cell-to-cell. The only reliable tactic — immersion/copious-water cooling — is precisely what a server room forbids to protect equipment. This is the unresolved doctrinal conflict the incident exposes: the verified >10h knockdown (ko.wikipedia) and reportedly ~22h full-out with repeated re-ignition (breached.company, secondary) are the direct symptom. No battery-level off-gas or thermal-runaway early warning was disclosed; the fire was human-noticed, not caught at cell venting.

Sourcing and confidence

High-confidence, verified facts (ko.wikipedia, re-fetched at review): ignition ~20:20 with unknown spark cause, first report ~20:15, worker powered off battery ~40 min prior, 647 systems shut, HVAC/CRAC loss, water-suppression difficulty, >10h knockdown, ~96 services interrupted, 1 worker 1st-degree burns, presumed UPS lithium-battery cause. Press-derived and NOT re-verifiable at review (fetch 403/404, search budget exhausted): 858 TB, no-backup rationale, ~125,000 officials, 5 negligence charges, restoration %, and all breached.company technical specifics (LG Energy Solution, 384 modules, Aug-2014 install, ~60 cm siting, >160°C, ~22h, ~101 firefighters, June-2025 inspection). These are retained as attributed, hedged secondary claims, not established fact.

Technical deep-dive
The event is a textbook demonstration of why lithium-ion energy storage in a server hall defeats the fire-protection assumptions data centers are built on. A conventional server-hall fire regime — clean-agent (inert gas or chemical clean agent) plus cautious or prohibited water application to protect equipment — is designed for combustion that needs external oxygen. A Li-ion cell in thermal runaway is self-oxidising: the decomposing cathode liberates oxygen internally, so smothering agents cannot starve it, and the reaction propagates cell-to-cell as each venting cell heats its neighbours. With the pack reported at 384 modules, once one cell failed during disconnection the runaway chained across the bank (breached.company, secondary). This is why the fire "reignited repeatedly" and why full extinguishment reportedly took ~22 hours after ignition, with initial knockdown alone taking over 10 hours — the >10h figure is verified in ko.wikipedia ("초진까지 10시간 이상이 소요되었다"), while the ~22h full-out and >160°C peak are press-derived secondary. The suppression dilemma is the central operational lesson. The room's fixed suppression did not arrest the event. ko.wikipedia (verified) states water-based firefighting was difficult because of concern over damaging the data-storage equipment, driving the >10-hour initial knockdown. But gas/clean agent cannot quench a self-oxidising runaway, and cautious water cannot rapidly cool cascading modules. The only reliable Li-ion tactic — total immersion / copious water cooling — was the one tactic the room's mission forbade. That unresolved conflict is what this incident exposes. The second dynamic is the de-energisation cascade. NIRS cut power to all 647 Daejeon systems ("647개 시스템"), partly a protective shutdown because the fire and response knocked out the room's HVAC/CRAC (항온항습장치); without cooling and dehumidification, running servers would overheat (ko.wikipedia, verified). So the outage was driven as much by the deliberate mass power-down and lost cooling as by flame — in a dense DC the blast radius of a single-rack event is defined by shared power and cooling topology, not the fire's physical footprint. Detection specifics (VESDA/aspirating, spot smoke/heat, or battery off-gas detection) and any automated-alarm time are NOT disclosed in any source; what is documented is a human-noticed first report at ~20:15 KST and ignition at ~20:20 (ko.wikipedia). No source indicates any battery-level gas or thermal-runaway early-warning system caught cell venting before flame — a detection gap that, for Li-ion, is the difference between a vented cell and a multi-hour fire.

Lessons learnt

  • A single-rack lithium-ion event defines its blast radius through shared power and cooling, not flame footprint: the 647-system outage came from the deliberate mass power-down and lost HVAC/CRAC as much as from the fire itself (ko.wikipedia, verified).
  • Server-hall fire doctrine is incompatible with Li-ion chemistry: water-cautious tactics could not arrest a self-oxidising runaway, producing the verified >10h initial knockdown (ko.wikipedia) and reportedly ~22h to full out with repeated re-ignition (breached.company). The one effective tactic — copious water/immersion cooling — is the one the room's mission forbids.
  • 'Too big to back up' is a decision, not a fact: ~858 TB was reportedly lost solely because G-Drive had no backup 'due to its large capacity' (Anadolu/DCD). Storage cost is always cheaper than total sovereign data loss.
  • A passed safety inspection is not proof of battery health: a reportedly passed June 2025 inspection preceded catastrophic runaway (breached.company); pass/fail inspection without cell-level off-gas/impedance/thermal diagnostics gives false assurance.
  • Human factors dominate: the fire is attributed to a disconnection during relocation with the ignition officially 'unknown' (ko.wikipedia) and police reportedly citing unqualified crew and illegal subcontracting (Anadolu) — governance of who touches energy storage matters as much as the hardware.
  • Cascade risk extends beyond the fire: recovery pressure was severe enough that an official overseeing restoration reportedly died amid overwork concerns (techspot) — recovery staffing and welfare must be planned, not improvised.

Improvements

  • Mandate hazardous-work permits, verified de-energization, thermal monitoring and physical isolation for all lithium-ion battery relocation and decommissioning, removing live server rooms from the work zone.
  • Relocate lithium-ion UPS energy storage into dedicated fire-rated battery rooms with early-warning gas/thermal detection and battery-appropriate suppression, separated from IT space.
  • Provision isolated, tested, recoverable backups and cross-site replication for shared government cloud repositories so no single fire can cause permanent multi-ministry data loss.
  • Distribute and geo-replicate critical national services across multiple facilities/availability zones to eliminate single-facility, single-event nationwide paralysis.
  • Compartmentalize and fault-isolate the facility (fire zoning, independent power and cooling per hall) so that a fire in one room does not require a precautionary shutdown of the entire co-located estate.
  • Institutionalize a pre-defined, drilled restoration runbook with priority tiers (Grade-1 services, cooling-first sequencing) and transparent progress reporting.
