Returns and income

IRR ?Internal Rate of Return — the discount rate at which NPV equals zero. Above your cost of capital means the project creates value. DC projects typically target IRR of 12-20%. Formula: solve NPV(IRR)=0 over the cashflow series.
--
Payback, years ?Simple payback period: years until cumulative net cashflow turns positive. Does not account for time value of money. Typical DC payback is 6-12 years. Shorter payback reduces investment risk and exposure to technology obsolescence.
--
Profitability index ?Ratio of NPV to initial investment (PI = 1 + NPV / CAPEX). PI above 1.0 signals value creation. Used to rank competing projects when capital is constrained. PI of 1.25 means every $1 invested returns $1.25 in present value.
--
ROIC ?Return on Invested Capital — peak-year NOI divided by total capital invested including CAPEX. Measures operational efficiency of deployed capital. A ROIC above WACC confirms the project earns above its cost of capital.
--
Peak revenue ?Maximum annual gross revenue at full stabilized occupancy. Equals IT capacity (kW) x utilization rate x colocation price per kW per month x 12. Drives the ceiling on NOI and terminal value.
--
Peak NOI ?Net Operating Income at stabilization — peak revenue minus total annual OPEX (energy, staffing, maintenance, insurance). Excludes debt service and depreciation. NOI is the primary driver of asset valuation using cap rate pricing.
--
Yield on cost ?Stabilized NOI divided by total project cost (CAPEX). Shows the unleveraged yield the project produces on cost. A yield-on-cost spread of 150-300 bps above the prevailing cap rate indicates a value-creation development margin.
--
Terminal value ?Estimated sale value of the asset at the end of the hold period. Calculated as stabilized NOI divided by the exit cap rate. Terminal value often represents 50-70% of total project return in DC investments held 10+ years.
--
