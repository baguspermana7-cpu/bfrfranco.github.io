Global Analysis · Investigation

The PFAS maintenance trap.

Data centers were told their cooling fluids were safe. The sealed-leak narrative missed the real vector — and the substitution chemistry quietly made it worse.

Apr 11, 202612 MIN READA26 · GLOBAL ANALYSIS

Maintenance vapor release, not sealed leaks, is the genuine PFAS exposure path. Every drain-and-fill cycle vents a fraction of the fluid inventory that the data sheets never accounted for — and the industry's safety case was built on the sealed system, not the service event.

01 The vapor nobody measured

The regulatory test measures a closed loop at steady state. The exposure happens during maintenance — when the loop is opened, drained, and refilled, venting vapor that no compliance sheet captures.

"The test measures the sealed system. The exposure happens during service."
— Field maintenance review, 2026
Key finding

"PFAS-free" is a label, not an outcome.

HFO refrigerants marketed as PFAS-free degrade into TFA — a persistent breakdown product that re-enters the same waterways the substitution was meant to protect.

The trap is structural, not accidental: the measurement boundary and the exposure boundary are different surfaces. Close the gap and the "safe" substitution stops looking safe.
