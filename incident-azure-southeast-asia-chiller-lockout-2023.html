Azure Southeast Asia 2023: Chillers Hang After a Dip, Then Lock Out

Microsoft Azure 2023-02-07 32h 11m to substantial restoration CoolingPower
SummaryTimelineResponseFMEACausesActionsSources

Summary

On 7 February 2023 a voltage dip tripped chillers at an Azure Southeast Asia zone in Singapore. Three chillers, over half the capacity, hung on a control-card fault the procedure did not cover; by the time it was reset the loop was too hot and they locked out. The zone was shut down from 20:19 UTC until the next day, and ARM and BCDR services failed with it.

Users affected
Customers in one Southeast Asia zone across about 30 services and the Azure portal; ARM management failures in Southeast Asia and East Asia; Site Recovery, Backup and GRS failover problems.

Root cause and contributing factors

TRIGGER: at 15:17 UTC on 7 February 2023 a utility voltage dip hit one Azure Southeast Asia Availability Zone in Singapore. Power systems rode through, but several chillers tripped. COOLING CHAIN: the plant had eight chillers from two suppliers. Chiller 5 was down for maintenance. Chillers 1–4 ran or restarted, but 6, 7 and 8, over half the capacity, would not start automatically or by the emergency procedure.
Full root-cause narrative
Their compressor control cards had stopped responding after the dip and needed a hard power-off of at least five minutes, which the procedure did not include. By the time the supplier's technicians had reset them, the loop water was above 28°C and the chillers locked themselves out. The zone's critical equipment was shut down at 20:19. PLATFORM CHAIN: the loss of one zone spread. ARM's metadata store was on a Cosmos DB instance wrongly configured as non-zonal, so management failed in the region; moving ARM traffic away overloaded East Asia. Site Recovery and Backup, the services meant for this situation, had their own non-zonal dependencies and configuration faults. LATENT ROOT: a chiller control fault without a documented recovery step, a plant already one chiller down, and resilience services that were not themselves resilient to a zone loss.

Contributing factors by category

  • A utility voltage dip tripped a subset of chillers (Azure PIR).
  • Compressor control cards on chillers 6–8 stopped responding and needed a cold reset that the emergency procedure did not list (Azure PIR).
  • Chiller 5 was already offline for planned maintenance, reducing spare capacity (Azure PIR).
  • Once the loop exceeded 28°C the chillers entered thermal lockout and could not restart (Azure PIR).
  • ARM's metadata store in the region was not zone-resilient because of a configuration error (Azure PIR).
  • Moving ARM traffic to East Asia exceeded its capacity, spreading impact to a second region (Azure PIR).
  • Site Recovery depended on a non-zonal Service Bus instance in the failed zone after a recent update (Azure PIR).
  • A BIOS bug and a Service Fabric memory issue kept one SQL tenant ring down until 04:30 on 9 February (Azure PIR).

Analysis

Half the plant would not start

Chillers 6–8 supplied 53% of capacity and hung on the same control-card fault. With chiller 5 in maintenance, the four running units could not hold the hall.

A missing line in the procedure

The fix, power the control card off for at least five minutes, was known to the OEM but not in the emergency procedure. Finding it cost the time in which the loop passed 28°C and locked the chillers out.

Lockout needs heat removed another way

Escaping lockout required temporary cooling and shutting equipment down. Temperatures were back in range only at 14:00 the next day.

Resilience services failed first

ARM, Site Recovery and Backup each had a configuration or dependency that tied them to the failed zone, so customers trying to execute their continuity plans hit errors, some in East Asia as well.

What the PIR does not say

It does not name the facility, the chiller suppliers or models, peak hall temperature, or cost. Most actions are reported completed; the Chaos Studio expansion was due December 2023.

Technical deep-dive
Chillers tripped by a voltage dip normally restart on their own. Here the compressor control cards hung after the dip and raised a phase sequence alarm; clearing it needed a cold restart with the capacitors drained. Each minute spent finding that out let the loop warm, and above 28°C the chillers' protection blocked any restart. Breaking out of lockout needed temporary cooling and less heat: both were done, and the chillers came back. The platform failures were configuration errors against Azure's own zone-redundancy rules: an ARM metadata store not zonal in this region, a Site Recovery workflow on a single-zone Service Bus, a Backup cross-region-restore setting, and a storage failover check that was too strict. Microsoft reports most of these fixed.

Lessons learnt

  • An emergency procedure is only as good as its rarest step; the cold reset the chillers needed was not written down.
  • Running a plant with a chiller out for maintenance removes the margin that would absorb a restart failure.
  • Thermal lockout turns minutes into hours; bring temporary cooling and shed load before the loop reaches the lockout limit.
  • Business-continuity services must be the most zone-resilient part of the platform, not the least.
  • Shifting a control plane to a neighbour region needs headroom there, or the failure moves with the traffic.
  • Zone-redundant workloads stayed largely available; regional services with hidden zonal dependencies did not.

Improvements

  • Cooling: OEM cold-restart step added to procedures, with training (Microsoft, completed).
  • Control plane: ARM zone redundancy validated and spare capacity in all regions (Microsoft, completed).
  • BCDR: Site Recovery workflow and Backup restore configuration fixed; storage failover checks adjusted (Microsoft, completed).
  • Architecture: daily dependency-graph walk for zonal services (Microsoft, ongoing).
  • Analyst recommendation (not in the PIR): treat a chiller out for maintenance as a reduced-redundancy state with a pre-staged temporary cooling plan.
