Google Cloud europe-west2-b Power Loss: PLC Logic Stops Generator Start

Google Cloud (facility operator not named) 2026-10-20 6h 8m to substantial restoration Powerconfig-error
SummaryTimelineResponseFMEACausesActionsSources

Summary

On 20 Oct 2026 a utility cable fault took out the only live feed to the UK facility hosting Google Cloud zone europe-west2-b, which was on a single feed during maintenance. The PLCs read live voltage on the line side of the open second feeder, so they never started the generators. The UPS ran out after about five minutes and the zone went dark for about 80 minutes; majority recovery took 6 hours 8 minutes.

Users affected
Google Cloud customers with workloads in europe-west2-b lost them for the outage; customers elsewhere in europe-west2 saw latency and errors during failover. No customer count is given.

Root cause and contributing factors

PROXIMATE CAUSE: an external underground cable fault took out primary utility feed A at 06:53 BST on 20 Oct 2026. Feed A was the only utility path carrying the site, because feeder B had been opened six days earlier for repairs found in a quarterly generator load test. WHY THE GENERATORS NEVER STARTED: the generator plant is started by a dual-redundant PLC.
Full root-cause narrative
Google's report says the PLC logic did not verify both an undervoltage condition and physical breaker position for both utility feeders. Its undervoltage sensing inputs were wired on the line side of the feeder breakers, and the start permissive only asked whether voltage was present there. Feed B was still energised upstream of its open breaker, so both the active and the standby master PLC, running identical logic on shared sensors, concluded a healthy source existed and did not issue a start command to the generator plant. WHY THE BATTERIES RAN OUT: the static UPS gives about five minutes of autonomy, sized for an automatic generator start and transfer, not for manual recovery. The onsite team tried a remote load transfer through the PLC, which the same wrong voltage reading blocked. The team then moved the site to the standby utility source, but the batteries were exhausted first and the data halls went dark at 07:00 BST. LATENT ROOT: a control-logic design and commissioning defect (sensing placed upstream of the isolation point, no breaker-status interlock) that only becomes dangerous in a maintenance line-up with one feeder breaker open. Redundant PLCs did not help because they shared the same logic and sensors. Nothing in the record shows the generator auto-start was tested in the 1N maintenance posture before the site was left in it; Google's new actions add exactly that test.

Contributing factors by category

  • Single-feed maintenance posture: after issues found in the 14 Oct 2026 quarterly generator load test, feeder B's breaker was opened for repairs, so feed A carried the whole critical load for six days (Google report).
  • PLC start logic checked voltage on the line side of the feeder breakers and did not check breaker position, so an open but upstream-energised feeder B read as a healthy source (Google report).
  • Common-mode redundancy: the active and standby master PLCs ran identical control logic on shared upstream sensors, so both made the same wrong decision (Google report).
  • UPS autonomy of about five minutes left no time for manual recovery once the PLC blocked the remote load transfer (Google report).
  • Testing gap (analyst inference from the new actions): the record shows no test of generator auto-start in the 1N posture before the site was left in it; Google's actions now require load-transfer tests 'within the backup battery timer'.
  • Customer failover was limited by capacity constraints in the surviving zones and by difficulty replicating the underlying storage (Google report, Compute Engine section).
  • Unclean power loss to storage forced a long, deliberate integrity recovery before compute could return (Google report).

Analysis

A correct-looking signal at the wrong point

The PLC did exactly what its logic asked. The defect is the measurement point: line-side voltage on a feeder whose breaker is open. In normal 2N operation that reading is harmless; in the maintenance posture it hid the loss of the only live feed. Control logic that infers 'supply healthy' from upstream voltage needs the breaker state in the same decision.

Redundancy that shared one mistake

The site had two utility feeders, a standby utility, a generator bank and two master PLCs. Three of those were unavailable or blind at once: feeder B was open by design, the generators were never asked to start, and both PLCs shared the same logic and sensors. Redundancy counted by box does not equal redundancy against a logic error.

The maintenance window as the real hazard

The trigger was ordinary, a utility cable fault. What made it a full outage was the six-day single-feed posture after a load-test finding. Google's response freezes similar maintenance elsewhere and adds approvals for non-standard configurations, which treats the line-up itself as a change that needs review.

Recovery dominated by storage, not power

Data-hall power was back at 08:20 BST, about 80 minutes after the halls went dark. Majority workload recovery came at 13:08. The gap is storage integrity work after an unclean shutdown and controlled traffic re-admission, a deliberate choice to protect data over speed.

What the record does not say

The report does not name the facility operator, the PLC manufacturer, the generator count or rating, the cost of the outage, or why the 14 Oct load-test issues required opening feeder B. Those gaps are stated here rather than filled in.

Technical deep-dive
The facility is designed 2N on utility (feeders A and B to separate intake switchboards), with a standby utility connection for manual campus-wide transfer and a central high-voltage generator bank. Start logic: on utility undervoltage the PLC starts and synchronises the diesel generators and transfers the load, inside the roughly five-minute UPS window. The failure is a permissive built on the wrong measurement point. A voltage transformer on the line side of an open breaker tells you the utility is alive, not that the load is fed. With feeder B open, the only correct signal that the critical bus had lost supply was feed A's undervoltage combined with feeder B's breaker position. The logic checked voltage alone, so the open breaker made the loss of the last live feed invisible. Duplicate PLCs running the same code on the same inputs are redundant against hardware failure but share every logic error: a common-mode failure. Downstream, the outage was a hard power loss, not an orderly shutdown. Storage that lost power uncleanly needed a long integrity recovery, and Google chose to hold services back rather than risk data. Customer-side zonal failover was partly undermined by capacity limits in the surviving zones and slow Regional Persistent Disk recovery. BigQuery's data-plane job could not scale up during failover because of an autoscaling dependency.

Lessons learnt

  • A start permissive must measure the bus it protects. Voltage on the line side of an open breaker proves the utility is alive, not that the load is fed: interlock voltage with breaker position.
  • Duplicated controllers are not diverse controllers. Two PLCs with the same code and the same sensors fail together on a logic error.
  • Every maintenance line-up is a new configuration. Before leaving a site in a reduced posture, test the automatic transfer in that exact line-up.
  • Five minutes of UPS is a window for automation, not for people. The manual fallback must be rehearsed to fit inside it, or the battery must be sized for it.
  • A hard power loss costs far more time in storage integrity recovery than the power outage itself: here about 80 minutes dark, over 6 hours to majority recovery.
  • Zonal failover only works if the surviving zones have capacity and the storage layer can actually fail over under load.

Improvements

  • Designverify both undervoltage and breaker position for each feeder before deciding a source is healthy (Google completed PLC reconfiguration).
  • Designreview PLC code for resilience to multiple simultaneous failures and validate the configuration at similar sites (Google committed).
  • Maintenance regime: full generator load-transfer tests that prove automatic start within the battery timer, repeated after the utility line-up is restored (Google completed and committed).
  • Maintenance regime: freeze planned maintenance at similar-topology sites and add elevated approval for non-standard configuration changes (Google completed).
  • Safety and operations: updated emergency procedure for loss of the active source, practised twice per shift (Google completed).
  • ProcessPLC code review inside commissioning and integrated systems testing before Facility Ready, with independent second-team validation (Google committed).
  • Analyst recommendation (not in the report): add a diverse, independent loss-of-supply detector on the critical bus that can start generators even if the master PLC logic is wrong.
